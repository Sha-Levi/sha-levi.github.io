Sam Parker, 2003–2013

On March 20, 2013 the Attorney General announced a State Grand Jury indictment: He was charged with four (4) counts of Misconduct in Office and two (2) counts of Furnishing Contraband to Inmates. The indictment alleged that Parker allowed convicted Department of Corrections inmates to live outside jail, go shopping, dine in public restaurants and wear civilian clothing in exchange for performing work on the Sheriff’s house and personal property. scag_parker_indict.html

The governor removed him the same week: Gov. Nikki Haley has suspended Chesterfield County Sheriff Sam Parker from office following his indictment on misconduct charges. Coroner Kip Kiser becomes the acting sheriff. wsoc_parker_indict.html

Prison Legal News summarized the 20-page indictment: Parker allegedly gave firearms owned or seized by the county to private citizens, including an M-14 rifle and a sniper rifle. It also reported that he was accused of using a boat purchased with county funds as a personal shrimping boat. pln_2013apr15_sc.html

At trial in April 2014 prosecutors showed the jury photographs of the room an inmate was allowed to furnish at the county armory. WSOC, describing them: He brought in big-screen TVs, a lazy boy chair, bought a new softer mattress and other furniture. On the stand, Parker admitted that he had never read the agreement between his office and the state concerning what inmates are and are not allowed to do. wsoc_parker_2inmates.htmlwsoc_parker_stand.html

The verdict: Parker was found guilty on five counts of misconduct in office, two counts of giving contraband to inmates and one count of embezzlement and was sentenced to 2 years in prison, with the possibility of parole. WCCB reported It took four hours for them to convict Parker on eight out of eight counts. WBTV reported that the jury deliberated for about three hours. wbtv_25278501.htmlwccb_28592.html

Parker admitted under oath he never read the agreement governing what inmates were allowed to do. That admission is the standard. The institution does not claim the rules were followed and the critic is wrong. It claims ignorance — feigned or real — as its defense. This pattern recurs across every sheriff in this section.

After sentencing: The former sheriff is being transferred to the state prison in Columbia. wccb_29285.html

The Ballot

The ballot question is one question with two authorizations: Must a special one percent sales and use tax be imposed in Chesterfield County, South Carolina for not more than eight (8) years, and $50 million in general obligation bonds, payable from an ad valorem tax imposed on all taxable property in Chesterfield County if the penny falls short. ballot_question.txt

A YES vote authorizes both. A NO vote denies both. A NO vote does not itself raise property taxes. Under S.C. Code §4-10-330(E), the tax is imposed only if a majority votes yes, and any later referendum on the question must be held at a general election.
