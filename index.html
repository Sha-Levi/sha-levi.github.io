Section 2

What the record says

Nine findings. Each figure is taken from the source named beneath it.

1

A man died in this facility

Death ruled a homicide · SLED investigation open

Gary "Bubba" Blair, 43, was found dead on the floor of his cell in E Pod on July 24, 2026 — 197 days after the January 8, 2026 SCDOC inspection.

  • Blair had been held since February on an impersonation charge.
  • 51 days before he died, a judge ordered the SC Office of Mental Health to place him in treatment. The placement never happened.
  • The autopsy ruled the manner of death a homicide. Cause: profound dehydration from deprivation of water.
  • Inmates told reporters that Blair drank water from his toilet and that other inmates passed him water in small chip bags through the food-tray slot. His cell was covered in feces; he had no mattress and no cover and was not allowed out. He wrote to his family and to a circuit court judge describing his conditions.
  • On July 29, five days after Blair was found, Sheriff Cambo Streater fired Jail Administrator Troy Ellerbe, whom he had appointed in 2023. The Sheriff has not said why.
  • SLED is investigating. No charges have been filed. The investigation is described as "open and active."

pennywithapurpose.com does not mention Gary Blair, the homicide ruling, or the SLED investigation.

Source: Queen City News, Jody Barr, Chief Investigative Reporter — September 4 and September 17, 2026. Homicide ruling confirmed by Coroner Kip Kiser (local press). Day count: January 8 to July 24, 2026 = 197 days.

3

$15.7 million missing from the Tax Account's books

Material weakness · repeat finding 2025-001

The county Treasurer's Tax Account collects every property tax payment in the county. The FY2025 audit found it had been kept so far behind that the books were materially wrong:

"…the Tax Account, which contains the largest volume of transactions, was missing approximately $2.5 million of revenue transactions and $13.2 million of expenditure transactions. This was identified when an outside accountant was hired by the County to assist in reconciling the tax account."FY2025 Single Audit, Finding 2025-001
  • A repeat finding. The same material weakness was reported the year before as Finding 2024-001. The auditor states the prior finding "was not corrected, and no partial correction was accomplished during the fiscal year."
  • The fix was promised a year earlier. In response to the FY2024 finding, the Treasurer wrote: "The deposit process within the Treasurer's Office will change from a manual one to a computerized one."
  • A year later, it had not changed. The FY2025 auditor recommended that "checks should be printed directly from the software instead of being hand written and manually keyed into the software at a later date."
  • $15.7 million is 64.8% of the county's FY2025–26 general fund operating budget ($24,236,849). The same budget funds an I.T./Computers department at $252,156.
  • Council's recorded response began when the audit deadline was missed. The FY2024 finding was presented on April 2, 2025 with no recorded discussion. On November 19, 2025 the Finance Committee voted unanimously to recommend "a full forensic audit of the Treasurer's Office" involving the State Treasurer's Office and SLED. On January 5, 2026 Council voted to "send a letter to the State Treasurer and the Governor stating Council's concerns with the leadership of the Chesterfield County Treasurer's Office."

If the tax passes, the revenue returns through this treasury. Under SC Code §4-10-360, within thirty days of each quarterly payment the county treasurer or the county administrator must certify to the Department of Revenue how much has been applied to project costs.

Source: FY2025 Single Audit, Schedule of Findings — Finding 2025-001 and Summary Schedule of Prior Audit Findings (Halliday, Schwartz & Co., Spartanburg, issued February 13, 2026); FY2024 Single Audit, Finding 2024-001 and client response; FY2025–26 Adopted Budget; Finance Committee minutes, Nov. 19, 2025; Council minutes, Jan. 5, 2026; SC Code §4-10-360.
4

The county is not a low-risk auditee

FY2025 Single Audit · 2 CFR §200.520

With the Treasurer's material weakness still open, the county spent $18,760,728 in federal awards in FY2025 and passed $13,722,717 of it to subrecipients.

  • $17,881,746 of the federal total was Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (ARPA), the only major program.
  • The Single Audit answers the question "Auditee qualified as low-risk auditee?" with No.
  • The auditor's opinion on federal-program compliance was unmodified, and there were no federal-award findings. The material weakness lies in the county's own financial reporting.
  • At June 30, 2025 the county's outstanding general obligation bonds totalled $1,956,109. The ballot asks for authority to issue $50,000,000, about 25 times that amount.
Source: FY2025 Single Audit — Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards and Summary of Auditor's Results; FY2025 Audited Financial Statements, long-term debt note.

6

The penny tax is not projected to cover the bond

$44,000,000 projected · $50,000,000 authorized

The Capital Project Sales Tax Commission projects about $5,500,000 a year, or $44,000,000 over eight years. The bond authorization is $50,000,000. That leaves a $6,000,000 gap, before interest.

The ballot says who pays the difference:

"…provided further that in the event the sales and use tax to be imposed as stated herein is inadequate for the payment of such bonds, such bonds shall be payable from an ad valorem tax imposed on all taxable property in Chesterfield County."Official Ballot, Referendum — November 3, 2026
  • A single Yes or No decides both the tax and the $50 million bond authority. Property owners are the stated backstop for anything the tax does not cover.
  • The ballot asks about bonds "including costs overruns, and issuance costs."
  • On May 18, 2026 the County Administrator gave Council two ways to fund the detention center: this sales tax, or a property tax millage increase.

The ballot does not offer an option to hold anyone accountable for the conditions that created the need.

Source: Official Ballot, Referendum — One Percent Sales and Use Tax and $50,000,000 General Obligation Bonds (published on pennywithapurpose.com); County Council minutes, May 18, 2026; CPST Commission revenue projection.

8

The website leaves out the operational violations

SC Code §24-9-20 · Minimum Standards for Local Detention Facilities

SCDOC inspection reports prepared under SC Code §24-9-20 list violations by Minimum Standards section number. The Minimum Standards cover how a jail is run as well as the building: staffing ratios, policy manuals, inmate classification, medical screening, sanitation, supervision, training and record-keeping.

The website shows three structural violations and no operational ones. A new building would not fix operational failures, because they have nothing to do with concrete and steel. Staffing, supervision, access to water, medical screening, inmate classification and compliance with court-ordered mental-health placements are not building problems.

The website's own FAQ lists "limited inmate classification space" among the facility's challenges. Classification is also an operational standard.

Source: SC Code §24-9-20; pennywithapurpose.com Facility Conditions and FAQs. The full inspection report can be requested with the first FOIA template in Section 4.
9

No email contact for referendum questions

Phone or in person only

The website's Contact page, which invites "further information regarding the proposed Detention Center referendum," lists two ways to ask: by phone, (843) 623-2535, or in person at 178 Mill Street, Chesterfield, SC 29709, Monday through Friday.

There is no email address for referendum questions. The only email on the site belongs to the Voter Registration & Elections office, for registration forms and absentee ballots.

A phone call leaves no record of what was asked or answered, while a written question does. Without an email option, the public has no easy way to create that record.

Source: pennywithapurpose.com Contact and Voting Information pages, as captured September 2026.

Section 5

The question the ballot does not ask

The ballot asks whether Chesterfield County should impose a 1% sales tax for up to eight years and issue $50,000,000 in general obligation bonds to build a new detention center.

It does not ask:

  1. Why was the current facility allowed to deteriorate in a year when non-grant revenue came in $4.1 million above budget?
  2. Why did the Detention Center exceed its budget by $384,819 with no corrective action?
  3. Why were $15.7 million in Tax Account transactions missing from the county Treasurer's books, a year after the same weakness was first reported?
  4. Why did a man die of dehydration 197 days after an inspection documented the facility's non-compliance?
  5. Who will be held accountable for the conditions that created the need for a $50 million replacement?
  6. Will the operational failures that produced those conditions follow the administration into the new building?

The people of Chesterfield County have the right to ask these questions, and to have them answered, before voting on November 3, 2026. The inspection report, the budget and the audit are all public records. The FOIA requests above are your right under SC Code §30-4-10. Use them.
